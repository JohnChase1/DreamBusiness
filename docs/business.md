# Dream Business — Business Context (for builds)

## Who we serve

Employed professionals, roughly 30–55, in Germany, Austria, UK, Romania, and the US. They have stable careers they do not want to blow up. They want to identify and launch the right business alongside the life they already have. Emotional register: they are capable and analytical, often skeptical of hustle culture and get-rich content. They respond to structure, evidence, and calm authority, not hype.

Design consequence: every interface should feel premium, calm, and trustworthy. Think Stripe or a well-made financial product, not a startup landing page with confetti. No urgency tricks, no countdown timers, no fake scarcity.

## Positioning beliefs (inform all copy)

- Skills first, opportunity second: start from what the person has done well, then find paying demand for it, not the other way around.
- The employed years are an asset, not a cage. We never disparage employment or push people to quit.
- Freedom is built on structure. Evidence before investment; proof thresholds before scaling.
- Comfort is the real competitor: the audience's alternative to buying is doing nothing, not buying elsewhere.

## Voice rules for any customer-facing text (condensed from Voice Corpus v2.0)

Hard constraints:
- No em dashes. Use commas, colons, or separate sentences.
- No exclamation marks, no slang, no hype language. Formal sentence construction.
- No first-person memoir, no invented clients, no fabricated metrics. Statistics carry sources. Illustrative examples are labeled illustrative.
- Institutional voice: "we" for framework statements. Never "I".

Banned vocabulary and patterns:
- delve, crucial, vital, landscape, navigate, leverage (as verb), robust, seamless, game-changer, unlock, harness, journey
- moreover, furthermore, additionally, "in today's fast-paced world"
- The contrast frame "it is not X, it is Y" and its variants (max one per long text, ideally zero)
- Rule-of-three parallel triplets as a rhythmic device (default to two items)
- Implicit metaphors the reader must decode; figurative language must explain itself in the same breath
- Bare two-beat aphorisms ("The difference sounds rhetorical. It is not.")

Style targets:
- Plain statements over clever ones. If a straight sentence and a clever one carry the same meaning, ship the straight one.
- Vary sentence and paragraph length. Long analytical sentences relieved by short declarative ones.
- Take positions; no hedging symmetry.

For long-form article generation, the full Voice Corpus v2.0 governs, not this summary.
